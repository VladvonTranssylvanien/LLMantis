You've been visited by the LLMantis checker

This page is what our User-Agent string points to. If a request from LLMantis-Checker/1.0 showed up in your server logs, here's exactly what happened and why.

What we did

We opened a public page on your site in an ordinary browser — the same Chromium an ordinary visitor uses, running your JavaScript and scrolling the page as a visitor would. If the entry page carried no chat widget, we may have followed your own Hilfe, Kontakt or Service link and looked there too. We were checking two things:

  • Is a chat widget present? We look at the requests the page makes, the objects its scripts install, and the elements it renders — not just at the raw HTML, because most widgets today are bundled into a site's own build and never show a vendor's domain at all.
  • If one is present: does its visible label, or the first thing the assistant says when the chat opens, disclose that it's an AI — as required by Art. 50(1) of the EU AI Act (in force since 2 August 2026)?

What we did not do

  • We did not send your bot a message and did not type anything. We clicked your chat button once — what any visitor does — and read the greeting your assistant produced on its own, because that greeting is what Art. 50(1) actually governs. Nothing was submitted, and no code path in the checker can enter text into a chat.
  • We did not run any attack against your assistant. That is a separate, paid test and it requires you to prove you own the domain first.
  • We never attempted to log in, bypass authentication, or access anything not already public. We did not accept a cookie banner on your behalf, and we do not disguise ourselves as something we're not.
  • We didn't store your page content. We keep whether a widget exists, the widget's visible label, and one screenshot.

What we will not claim

If we could not open your widget — a cookie wall, a login, a button that would not respond — our result says not determinable and nothing more. We do not infer non-compliance from a button we never opened, and we do not infer it from page copy either. A missing disclosure is only reported when we actually read what your assistant said and it did not say it was one.

Who we are

LLMantis tests whether AI chatbots disclose that they're AI and, for consenting customers, runs documented security attacks against them. This particular check — the free layer — asks no permission because it does nothing a visitor could not do: it loads public pages and clicks one public button. The line we hold is between reading what your assistant says and writing to it. We read. Attacking it is the paid test, and it requires you to prove the domain is yours.

About robots.txt

We read your robots.txt and record what it says on every check, and the result names it. We do not treat it as a bar to opening one public page: it is a voluntary convention (RFC 9309) addressed to crawlers that collect content, and this is a single compliance read of a page any visitor can open. We are not copying your content, indexing it, or using it to train anything.

How to opt out

Write to us and we will stop, permanently, and tell you what we already hold about your site. A human reads every message: kontakt@llmantis.de. Blocking the X-LLMantis-Checker header or our address at your edge also works, and we won't route around it.
